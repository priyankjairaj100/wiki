\section*{Limitations}

The certificates are exact relative to supplied pair decisions and timestamps.
They do not verify source truth or infer effective dates from publication metadata.
Concurrent values require replacement decisions that distinguish coexistence from retirement.
Our current matcher does not solve that general extraction problem.

The new experiments use small English collections and a fixed lexical ranking.
Wikidata and TempLAMA claims are rendered from structured records.
RealProse contains genuine extracts but curated tenure dates.
Its 35 subjects provide limited coverage of open-domain prose.
The complete TempLAMA answer-set analysis is a development evaluation with frozen rankings and no subsequent parameter tuning.
Its retrieval pool still contains only the previously rendered selected answers.
It therefore does not evaluate exhaustive retrieval of every valid concurrent answer.

Direct invalidation is an existing temporal-memory operation.
Our comparison formalizes its behavior under shared pair decisions.
The experiments compare matched compilation rules, rather than complete Graphiti, MemStrata, or Re$^3$ pipelines.
The archived reader runs evaluate an unchanged current-answer path.
New historical results measure retrieved evidence rather than generated answers.

The stability theorem fixes relevance scores and timestamps.
One document edit can change several pair decisions or alter the ranking.
One changed evidence item can also change a generated answer.
The certificate guarantee bounds structural propagation, rather than downstream answer sensitivity.
