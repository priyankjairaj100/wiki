\section*{Limitations}
The exactness results assume independent occurrence-date bounds and fixed directed replacement decisions.
Correlated dates can make the product of bounds include impossible timelines.
The resulting masks remain conservative, but need not be sharp.
Incorrect extraction can supply incorrect bounds or replacement decisions.
The compiler guarantees execution of those inputs, rather than their semantic correctness.
It handles questions asking for support somewhere within a period.
Universal coverage throughout a period requires a different query predicate.

The independent oracle validates the compiler on exhaustive finite configurations and selected fractional endpoint cases.
The mathematical proofs establish the general result.
The corpus experiments measure full-answer snapshot selection and natural-passage retrieval.
They do not yet measure the new uncertainty compiler inside a learned claim-extraction and answer-generation pipeline.
The TimeQA baselines are source-only executor diagnostics.
They are not reproductions of complete DeFuzzRAG, IA-RAG, or NuggetIndex systems.

TempLAMA is a structured diagnostic benchmark with template-rendered claims.
Its annual answer sets can include successive values within one year.
TimeQA supplies natural passages, but some answer anchors are aliases or mentions outside the relevant event description.
Annotated-span retrieval differs from temporal entailment and generated-answer correctness.
The human questions overlap the larger TimeQA test collection.
Our source audit removes empty positive labels and separates development articles from test articles.

Archived reader outputs use the original selected-answer task.
No new reader run supports the claims in this revision.
The older RealProse excerpts contain source-support problems documented in the appendix.
The release keeps those results as historical records.
They do not serve as primary natural-language evidence for the new compiler.
