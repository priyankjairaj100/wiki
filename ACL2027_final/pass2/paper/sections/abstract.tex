\begin{abstract}
Sources often date an update only to a month or year.
Historical questions can also cover entire periods.
Assigning exact dates can then hide uncertainty and discard useful evidence.
We introduce a temporal retrieval compiler that preserves the source's date constraints.
It stores at most two replacement records for each claim.
These records distinguish evidence that could apply from evidence that must apply somewhere within the requested period.
We prove that two records suffice and can be necessary.
We also identify exactly when date uncertainty cannot change the ranked claim context.
Each mistaken directed replacement decision affects only its target claim.
Independent oracles match all 253,050 tested support decisions and 328,747 context-stability decisions.
Experiments preserve complete TempLAMA answer sets and original TimeQA paragraphs.
A latest-value baseline suppresses 13.63\% of accepted annual answers by keeping only one value.
The results establish an exact selection interface and a source-grounded protocol for temporal evidence.
\end{abstract}
