\section{Source-Derived Replacement Challenge}
\label{app:replacement}

\paragraph{Selection before outcomes.}
We screened the saved development and test paragraphs without reading original questions or answer labels.
We first excluded 332 articles from the documented exposure records.
Candidates required two distinct calendar years and an explicit succession cue.
The cues were \emph{succeeded}, \emph{successor}, \emph{replaced}, \emph{replacing}, \emph{replacement}, \emph{took over}, and \emph{handed over}.
We ordered 496 candidates by SHA-256 of a fixed seed and passage identifier.
The first 128 paragraphs yielded seven accepted graphs.
Before certificate evaluation, we fixed one additional panel containing the next 256 paragraphs.
This panel yielded thirteen accepted graphs.
The expansion responded to annotation yield, without changing eligibility rules.
All 384 judgments remain available, including rejections and uncertain cases.

Two model-assisted reviews checked every accepted graph against its complete source paragraph.
Reviewers divided the additional annotation work and independently checked each other's proposed graphs.
We excluded graphs with uncertain dates, roles, or replacement relations.
We also excluded named prior examples and one article inspected during the current editorial audit.
Membership of an earlier lost audit remains incomplete, so this challenge does not claim fully unseen articles.
The accepted panel contains seventeen test paragraphs and three development paragraphs.
These reviewed graphs form a mechanism diagnostic, rather than an evaluation of automatic extraction.

\paragraph{Graphs and source excerpts.}
Each graph retains its complete paragraph, article identifier, literal offsets, and date provenance.
Twenty-five edges record consecutive replacements.
Eight edges connect nonconsecutive holders within complete, explicitly ordered succession lists.
We tag these closure edges separately.
Source succession also supplies strict order constraints between the corresponding dates.
A transition stated as both departure and succession uses one shared event, avoiding an independent duplicate endpoint.
Separate explicit departures retain their own bounds.
All 51 calendar dates and 174 literal spans pass source checks.
Displayed excerpts contain literal clauses or coherent clause segments.
Excerpts can overlap because one sentence can describe both holders.

\paragraph{Queries and controls.}
Each successor contributes its earliest date, integer midpoint, and latest date as point queries.
We remove duplicate dates within each graph, producing 73 queries.
The predicate is overlap, with $a=b$.
The fixed ranking follows start-mention offsets, with annotation order breaking ties.
One fixed fallback excerpt $F$ follows every modeled claim.
We evaluate budgets $k=1$ and $k=5$.
Both policies retain identical bounds, explicit ends, and known source orders.
The interval control removes only cross-claim replacement edges.
Thus the comparison isolates replacement while preserving the same feasible calendar assignments.

\paragraph{Exact evaluation and replay.}
The oracle searches every integer calendar assignment through complete difference-constraint branching, as described in Appendix~\ref{app:constrained-dates}.
Endpoint sampling does not supply the reference labels.
The source-order comparison changes 33 selected contexts at $k=1$ and 34 at $k=5$.
Only the first budget changes stability labels, with 42 interval-stable contexts becoming unstable.
Replacement certifies 25 queries at each budget.
All 96 unstable context decisions have two feasible timelines that obey every source order and change the literal excerpts.

We also retain the independent-date comparison on the same graphs and queries.
The linear compiler matches 388 exact support judgments across 194 claim-query pairs.
The lazy selector matches all 146 context decisions.
All 98 unstable decisions have replayed witnesses under that product model.
These counts include both budgets and do not represent independent source questions.

\paragraph{Effect of a real source order.}
The Levadia paragraph dates Bondarenko's appointment to 2001 and Rautiainen's succession to November 2001.
Their known order removes three possible-support judgments for Bondarenko.
The affected queries are 30 November 2001, 1 January 2003, and 16 January 2003.
This restriction resolves one additional stable query at each budget.
The archive retains both comparisons, every source decision, and every replayed timeline.
