\begin{abstract}
Archives contain relevant statements that describe different periods.
Temporal retrieval must select evidence that applies to the requested date.
An incorrect connection between claims can also change earlier evidence when a system merges their histories.
We study this propagation problem through direct supersession certificates.
Each certificate stores the first later claim accepted as a replacement by a supplied pairwise matcher.
We characterize this operation when matching decisions do not form consistent groups.
One certificate per claim reproduces replacement decisions at every requested date.
Later-dated additions preserve earlier eligibility decisions.
Changing one pair affects at most one claim interval.
With a fixed ranking, the selected set changes by at most one removal and one insertion.
An exact compiler produces these certificates without gold entity or value annotations.
Component timelines change past eligibility at 8 of 11 TempLAMA event times and 27 of 42 Wikipedia event times.
Direct certificates have no such changes.
Complete TempLAMA answer sets give 94.7\% current evidence accuracy, compared with 88.3\% for exact-group date selection.
Historical stale-context exposure falls from 16.6\% to 1.8\%, with historical accuracy of 98.2\% versus 98.4\%.
The results connect formal stability to source-attributed evidence selection.
\end{abstract}
