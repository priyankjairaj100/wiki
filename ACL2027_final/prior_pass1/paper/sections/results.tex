\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Later records can change earlier component states}

Table~\ref{tab:prefix} tests temporal prefix consistency using observed records.
Component intervals disagree with prefix-only compilation at 8 of 11 TempLAMA event times.
They disagree at 27 of 42 RealProse event times.
Direct certificates have zero disagreements on both collections.
Wikidata has no disagreement under either compiler.
These results identify a structural failure that depends on the observed pair graph.
\input{tables/prefix}

The differences can reach the retrieved evidence.
Full-corpus versus prefix-only component compilation changes the first result for six TempLAMA historical probes and eight RealProse probes.
The RealProse versions have equal aggregate Hit@1 despite those different selections.
Thus aggregate accuracy alone does not detect this instability.
The certificate guarantee applies at each cutoff and record, rather than only to an average score.

\subsection{One pair can affect several component intervals}

Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} removes accepted pairs from the observed corpora.
Direct compilation changes at most one interval in every intervention.
The corresponding maxima for component compilation are four on Wikidata, three on TempLAMA, and eight on RealProse.
The intervention set contains both correct and incorrect pairs.
It measures sensitivity to a changed decision, rather than treating every removed pair as an error correction.
\input{tables/sensitivity}

Cross-key pairs provide a separate error-focused analysis.
TempLAMA contains 46 accepted cross-key pairs; RealProse contains 96.
Removing one such pair changes up to three component intervals in either collection.
Across RealProse event times, these corrections produce 68 component membership changes and 37 direct membership changes.
Several pair changes leave direct endpoints unchanged because another witness establishes the same first boundary.
The measured influence agrees with Theorem~\ref{thm:influence}.

\subsection{Evidence accuracy and stale exposure}

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} reports full-pool retrieval with matched relevance scores.
Its main TempLAMA rows use complete original answer sets.
Direct certificates reach historical Hit@1 of 0.982, compared with 0.781 for BM25.
Exact-group latest reaches 0.984 and component intervals reach 0.975.
The direct--component difference is 0.0067, with a 95\% interval of $[-0.0023,0.0167]$.
The direct--exact-group difference is $-0.0022$, with interval $[-0.0183,0.0153]$.
These close accuracy results accompany different evidence exposure.
\input{tables/retrieval}

Historical Stale@5 is 0.709 for BM25, 0.166 for exact grouping, and 0.018 for direct certificates.
Component intervals also reach 0.018.
Direct certificates preserve historical Hit@5 of 0.991, while exact grouping reaches 1.000.
The comparison therefore measures a tradeoff between retaining valid alternatives and excluding obsolete evidence.
For current TempLAMA queries, direct and component selection reach Hit@1 of 0.947.
Exact grouping reaches 0.883.
Their difference is 0.0633, with interval $[0.0333,0.0933]$.

RealProse tests the same operations on genuine prose.
Historical Hit@1 rises from 0.567 for BM25 and exact grouping to 0.835 for direct and component intervals.
The direct gain over exact grouping is 0.268, with interval $[0.161,0.378]$.
Direct Hit@5 is 0.938, compared with 0.918 for component intervals.
Their Stale@5 values are 0.485 and 0.381, respectively.
The direct rule preserves some evidence that component closure removes.
Its bounded influence does not imply lower stale exposure in every corpus.

On the source-defined single-answer subset, direct certificates recover 55 of 57 historical first results.
Exact grouping recovers all 57; component intervals recover 54.
Direct and component Stale@5 are both 0.018, compared with 0.193 for exact grouping.
This subset confirms that stale exposure and first-result accuracy measure different properties.

\subsection{Exact compilation and current-mask preservation}

The compiler matches exhaustive direct evaluation on all packaged corpora.
The checks cover endpoints, selected witnesses, eligibility cutoffs, timestamp ties, and missing timestamps.
They also verify exact equality with the archived current mask.
Table~\ref{tab:compiler} reports the main corpora's comparison counts.
\input{tables/compiler}

For Wikidata, the indexed compiler tests 2,738 pairs against 19,981 strictly ordered pairs in the exhaustive scan.
For TempLAMA, the counts are 26,538 and 241,154.
For RealProse, the indexed compiler tests 2,876 pairs.
These reductions preserve the full set of earliest boundaries.
They measure this matcher and these corpora, while Equation~\ref{eq:cost} retains the general worst-case cost.

The preserved current masks also preserve previously generated contexts under the archived ranking.
Appendix~\ref{app:legacy} records the inherited reader results and their original scoring task.
Those runs assess the unchanged current-answer path.
The new results in this section assess compiler behavior and evidence selection.
